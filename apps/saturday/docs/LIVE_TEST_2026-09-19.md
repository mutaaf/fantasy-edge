# The first real Saturday: 18–19 September 2026

The first run against football as it happened, rather than against a
recording. What follows is what it proved, what it found, and what it did not
get to. `docs/LIVE_TEST.md` is the plan; this is the result.

## What ran

| | |
|---|---|
| Slate | 74 FBS games: 3 Friday, 71 Saturday |
| Recorded | 16:49 Sat → 02:46 Sun ET, 557 boards, 74 finals, 2,768 live snapshots |
| Recorder errors | 0. It stopped itself: "every slate game is final and saved" |
| Backfilled | Friday 19:36 → Saturday 16:49 ET, 572 reconstructed frames |
| Merged night | **1,129 frames, Friday 19:36 → Sunday 02:46 ET**, continuous |
| Requests | one scoreboard per cycle plus the watched six; `within` budget all night |

The recorder was started late - 16:49, after Friday's three games and the
whole early afternoon - which is why half this page is about backfilling. That
turned out to be the most useful accident of the night.

## What it proved

- **A day nobody recorded can be rebuilt.** Every ESPN play carries the
  instant it happened, so the missing hours were reconstructed from the
  summaries afterwards. Held against the recorder's own boards where the two
  overlap: **1,387 of 1,416 game-moments identical (97.9%)**, and every one of
  the 29 differences explained (below).
- **The budget holds with several clients.** Three clients against live ESPN -
  two polling, one streaming with two games open - took 13 scoreboard requests
  in 253 s against a limit of 13, serving 228 polls.
- **The recorder runs unattended.** It slept through the overnight lull, kept
  the Mac awake, rotated its log, wrote a heartbeat every cycle, and ended on
  its own after the last final.

## What it found

### ESPN's play feed leads its own scoreboard

The single finding of the night, and it only shows up when a rebuild is held
against a recording of the same minute. All 29 differences are this:

| Kind | Count | What it looks like |
|---|---:|---|
| `playFeedAheadOfBoard` | 26 | WKU–Indiana at 20:51:14Z: the board said 0–6, the play already said 0–7 |
| `startsAtItsFirstPlay` | 2 | Florida State–Alabama's kickoff was stamped before ESPN flipped the board off "scheduled" |
| `endsAtItsLastPlay` | 1 | Maine–Boston College read Final while the board still showed 0:36 in the 4th |

A touchdown's play carries the score **after** the extra point, so a rebuild
never passes through the six-point moment a live board shows for thirty
seconds. A game begins at its first play and ends at its last, not when the
board caught up.

None of this is recoverable, and none of it is hidden:
`tools/verify_reconstruction.py` classifies a difference rather than counting
it, and fails only on one it cannot explain. On this night: none.

### The closing-frame bug

The recorder writes its last board as `<stamp>-closing.json.gz`. A capture
listed its frames by stamp and then read `<stamp>.json.gz`, so scrubbing to
the end of a finished night raised `FileNotFoundError`. Only a night that
actually ended could show it; the 12 September capture never had one. Fixed,
with a test.

### What a rebuild cannot know

Carried in every rebuilt board and in the app's own note, not only here:

- a score corrected and then corrected back reads as one clean change;
- the two weather delays in progress at 16:49 (UNC–Clemson, Mississippi
  State–South Carolina) are simply absent from the rebuilt hours;
- halftime is inferred from the gap between periods, not read from a board;
- a few ESPN wallclocks are wrong by hours - one Buffalo–Penn State play is
  stamped two hours after the game ended - so the play order is trusted over
  the stamps.

## What is still untested

- **The LAN device passes.** The iPhone and Vision Pro checklist in
  `docs/LIVE_TEST.md` was never run against a live slate: the app was only
  driven against replays in the simulator. It needs a live Saturday, so it is
  deferred to the next one.
- **The 3D volume and stadium** remain placeholders until fantasy-edge's scene
  branch lands.
- **A delay seen live.** Two games were delayed before the recorder started;
  none began a delay while it was running, so the delay path has still only
  been exercised against the 12 September recording.
