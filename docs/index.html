Three things it does

None of them is “more stats”.

Every league on one screen

A Sleeper league and an ESPN league are two apps that will never speak to each other, so on a Sunday you tab between them and compare from memory. Here they are rows on the same board — one place to answer the only question that actually matters: out of everything happening right now, what should I be watching?

Sized by what is still in doubt

Not by what is big. A back with a half still to play in a one-score game fills the screen; a matchup already decided shrinks away on its own. That is arithmetic, recomputed as the games move, not a layout somebody chose — and it is a couple of hundred lines you can read all of.

Where you actually watch football

This browser. Your phone, laid out for a thumb. The television across the room. And a native Vision Pro app that puts the game itself in the middle of your line-up. One model, ported by hand to each surface and checked against itself, so no screen waits on a server to be told how big to draw a cell.

Questions

Including the two nobody asks out loud.

How do I find my Sleeper username? It's public

It is the display name your leaguemates see — the one on your avatar in the Sleeper app — not the email address you sign in with. That name is all this page asks for, and it is all Sleeper's public read API needs to list the leagues you are in.

Where do I find my ESPN league id? In the URL

Open your league on ESPN and read the address bar. It contains leagueId= followed by a run of digits, and those digits are the whole of it.

Paste them here with the season and this page asks ESPN directly, with no credential attached. If the league's visibility is public, ESPN answers. If it is private it returns 401, and you get the next question rather than an error code.

Do I need an account, a login or a password? No

No, and there is nothing to sign up to. This page is a file on a static host: no backend, no database, no session. Everything on it is fetched by your browser, directly from the provider.

Sleeper needs your username. A public ESPN league needs a league id. That is the entire list.

Will this page ever ask for my password, espn_s2 or SWID? Never

No — not now, and not once the extension below ships. espn_s2 and SWID are your live ESPN session: anyone holding those two cookies can read and change your whole ESPN account, not just look at your league.

A public web page that asks a stranger to paste a session cookie is indistinguishable from a page built to steal one, and it teaches exactly the habit phishing depends on. This project's own rules forbid a credential ever touching the repository, and a page served from the repository is not an exception.

So there is no input for one on this page, and there never will be. If you ever meet a page wearing this one's name and asking for those two cookies, it is not this one.

My ESPN league is private. What now? Coming soon

Coming soon: a browser extension. ESPN gives third parties no way to sign you in — no OAuth, no read-only key, nothing to apply for. The one legitimate route left is an extension that reads your own, already-signed-in session on ESPN's own domain and never carries it anywhere else. That is how FantasyPros reaches a private league, and it is the route this is taking.

Today, a private league already works on your own machine, where the cookie stays in your own environment and never leaves it: python3 -m fantasyedge api. See the quickstart. That is not a lesser option — it is the whole app, with history, analytics and the draft board.

Why is there no Yahoo button? Coming soon

Yahoo is coming; it is waiting on Yahoo. Their Fantasy Sports API now goes through a manual review before an application is approved, and this one is in that queue. The OAuth client is written and covered by tests; the review is the only thing left. No date is promised here, because nobody has one to promise.

There is a second reason the card is not clickable yet. OAuth needs a client secret, and a static page has nowhere to keep one: every byte shipped to your browser is readable by everyone who loads it. Publishing it would let anyone impersonate this application, and Yahoo would revoke it inside a day. So when Yahoo does arrive here, it arrives with the token exchange running somewhere a secret can actually live — not with a secret baked into this file.

Already have Yahoo API credentials of your own? The app on your own machine can use them today, where a secret can live in your environment: python3 -m fantasyedge auth --provider yahoo --url.

Where does my data go? Nowhere

Nowhere, because there is nowhere to send it. This page has no server behind it. Your username, league id and team choice are written to this browser's localStorage and read by nothing but this page.

No analytics, no cookies, no account. Clearing your browser data, or pressing Forget this browser on the board, erases the connection completely.

Which hosts does my browser contact? Five

These, and nothing else. A test in the repository asserts this list against the page itself, so a stray CDN or tracker added later fails the build rather than quietly appearing here.

api.sleeper.app — your Sleeper leagues, rosters, live points
site.web.api.espn.com — the public NFL slate, scores and box scores
lm-api-reads.fantasy.espn.com — a public ESPN league, unauthenticated
fonts.googleapis.com / fonts.gstatic.com — typefaces

No server, no account, no analytics. This page is a file, and the questions above say exactly what it asks for, where that is kept and what it will never ask for at all.
